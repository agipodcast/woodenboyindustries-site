One passage, three reading levels

Copy this prompt into a new Gemini chat and press Enter.

Starter prompt · school account · approved tool · parent-proof Context: I teach 3rd grade. Below is a reading passage from our plant life cycle unit. Role: Act as a reading specialist. Action: Rewrite the passage at three reading levels: easier, on grade level, and harder. Keep every fact in all three versions, and don't add new ones. Keep these words: germinate, seedling, pollen, pollination, fruit, life cycle. Format: Label them Version A, Version B, and Version C, and tell me which is which at the end. Make the easier version easier with shorter sentences and simpler words, not by leaving facts out. It can be shorter than the others. Bold the vocabulary words. Tone: Clear and friendly, for 8-year-olds. Passage: From Seed to Seed: The Life of a Bean Plant A bean plant starts as a small, hard seed. It may not look alive, but it is. Inside the seed is a tiny baby plant. The seed also holds food for the baby plant. A tough seed coat covers the outside and keeps it safe. A seed can wait a long time. To start growing, it needs water, air, and warmth. When a bean is planted in damp, warm soil, it soaks up water and swells. Then the seed coat splits open. The seed begins to germinate. That means it starts to grow. First, a root pokes out of the seed and grows down into the soil. Roots hold the plant in place. They also soak up water. Next, a stem pushes up through the soil. Soon a little green plant pops out of the ground. Now it is called a seedling. By now, the seedling has used up its stored food. Its green leaves make new food from sunlight, air, and water. The stem grows taller. It carries water from the roots up to the leaves. In a few weeks, the seedling grows into an adult plant. An adult bean plant grows flowers. Each flower makes a yellow dust called pollen. Moving pollen so a flower can make seeds is called pollination. A bean flower can pollinate itself. Bees help too. They visit flowers to drink sweet nectar, and pollen sticks to their fuzzy bodies. When a bee flies to the next flower, some pollen rubs off. After a flower is pollinated, it changes. The petals dry up and fall off. A long green pod grows in its place. The pod is the bean plant's fruit. A fruit is the part of a plant that holds its seeds. Inside the pod, new seeds grow bigger every day. At the end of summer, the plant turns brown and dies. The pods dry out and split open. Some seeds fall to the ground. A gardener might save others in a jar. Next spring, one of those seeds can be planted. It will germinate, grow, flower, and make new seeds. This pattern is called a life cycle. It goes from seed to seed, again and again.

What good looks like

  • The facts match the original in all three versions. Nothing new was added.
  • The easier version uses shorter sentences, not babyish ones.
  • The harder version uses longer sentences and linking words like because and so. The facts stay the same.
  • The labels are Version A, B, and C, so students never see a level label.

Make it better
